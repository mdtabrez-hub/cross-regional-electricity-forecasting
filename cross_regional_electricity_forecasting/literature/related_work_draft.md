**Load forecasting benchmarks and the probabilistic turn.** Short-term load forecasting has a long history of point prediction. Hong and Fan's tutorial review [hong2016probabilistic] set out the case for probabilistic forecasts and their evaluation. The Global Energy Forecasting Competitions made zonal hierarchical forecasting [hong2014gefcom2012], quantile-based probabilistic scoring [hong2016gefcom2014] and hierarchical probabilistic forecasting with external data [hong2019gefcom2017] standard benchmarks. Later reviews highlight reproducibility and open data as priorities [hong2020energy]. These benchmarks, however, are within one system: models are trained and tested on zones of the same utility or ISO. Robustness to *temporal* shift has received more attention since the COVID-19 pandemic. Adaptive state-space and online-aggregation methods won a post-COVID day-ahead competition [farrokhabadi2022dayahead]. Kalman-filter-adapted additive models improved forecasts during lockdowns, including through transfer from another country [obst2021adaptive]. These were extended to adaptive probabilistic forecasting via online quantile regression on residuals, evaluated on British regional net-load and U.S. city demand [devilmarest2024adaptive]. These studies address change over time within known systems. They do not address deployment to systems absent from training.

**Global versus local models.** Global models, fitted jointly across many series, can match the expressiveness of local models without assuming the series are similar [montero2021principles]. Pooled gradient boosting featured prominently in large forecasting competitions such as M5 [makridakis2022m5], LightGBM [ke2017lightgbm] is a common implementation, and simulation evidence suggests pooled LightGBM stays competitive on heterogeneous, short series [hewamalage2022global]. Global deep probabilistic models such as DeepAR [salinas2020deepar] and N-BEATS [oreshkin2020nbeats] are common neural alternatives. For electricity load, Ahmadi et al. report that global models outperform local ones on Alberta data and include a zero-shot evaluation on regions not used for training [ahmadi2025globalization]. In a non-energy setting, Ling and Wu compare global, clustered and local probabilistic LightGBM models using coverage and interval width [ling2025comparative]. They find unit identifiers useful, but identifiers are by construction unavailable for an unseen region. Most global-versus-local comparisons evaluate series that were pooled during training. Generalisation to withheld series is less often isolated, and its effect on uncertainty calibration is rarely reported.

**Transfer and few-shot learning across regions.** Several studies transfer load models between regions. Tzortzis et al. pre-train neural networks on European national demand series and adapt them to held-out countries, finding gains over per-country training, particularly when source countries are clustered [tzortzis2024transfer]. Yuan et al. frame cross-regional forecasting as a regional distribution-shift problem and propose a meta-learning initialisation that adapts to unseen German TSO areas [yuan2026adaptive]. Hierarchical transfer from fine-grained to aggregate load [antoniadis2024hierarchical] and meta-learning for data-scarce consumers [tsoumplekas2025fewshot] address related settings. At building level, cross-building transfer dates back at least to seasonal- and trend-adjusted data merging [ribeiro2018transfer], and BuildingsBench established zero-shot and fine-tuning evaluation on unseen buildings after large-scale synthetic pre-training [emami2023buildingsbench]. These works mostly report point accuracy on a few target regions or on building-level data. Whether transfer preserves *calibrated* uncertainty for large, independently operated systems remains largely unexamined.

**Time-series foundation models.** Pre-trained forecasters such as Chronos [ansari2024chronos], TimesFM [das2024decoder], Moirai [woo2024unified], Lag-Llama [rasul2023lagllama] and TimeGPT [garza2023timegpt] claim competitive zero-shot accuracy on unseen datasets. For household load, zero-shot foundation models perform comparably to Transformers trained from scratch [meyer2025benchmarking]. On ERCOT system load, a recent preprint reports that interval calibration differs sharply between foundation models, with some close to nominal and others markedly overconfident [simeone2026tsfm]. A separate preprint benchmarks deep sequence models on EIA-930 balancing-authority demand with per-grid training [hong2026benchmarking]. Calibration of zero-shot models therefore cannot be assumed, and possible overlap between pre-training corpora and public load data complicates zero-shot claims. Both points motivate a model-agnostic recalibration layer.

**Probabilistic evaluation and conformal prediction under shift.** We evaluate forecasts with proper scoring rules [gneiting2007strictly] under the principle of maximising sharpness subject to calibration [gneiting2007probabilistic], using quantile (pinball) losses that originate in quantile regression [koenker1978regression]. Conformal prediction [vovk2005algorithmic] and its split variant [lei2018distribution] give distribution-free marginal coverage under exchangeability. Conformalized quantile regression adds heteroscedastic adaptivity [romano2019conformalized], and weighted conformal prediction handles covariate shift when a likelihood ratio can be estimated [tibshirani2019conformal]. For dependent and drifting data:

- adaptive conformal inference (ACI) updates the target miscoverage online and guarantees long-run coverage [gibbs2021adaptive];
- AgACI removes ACI's learning-rate choice through expert aggregation and was demonstrated on electricity prices [zaffran2022adaptive];
- weighted conformal methods bound the coverage loss under non-exchangeability [barber2023conformal];
- strongly adaptive online conformal methods target changing regimes [bhatnagar2023improved];
- EnbPI [xu2021conformal] and SPCI [xu2023sequential] exploit residual structure;
- conformal PID control [angelopoulos2023conformal] and online-optimisation approaches [areces2025online] report experiments on electricity demand.

Applied studies have used conformalized quantile regression for transmission-level load [matulin2023cqr]. Electricity applications of online conformal methods have so far mostly involved single series and temporal drift. Coverage under *geographic* shift, where a model is calibrated on some regions and deployed on a region it has never seen, is less studied for system-level load. So is the cold-start period in which an adaptive method has little target feedback.

**Data quality and evaluation methodology.** EIA-930 provides hourly demand and day-ahead demand forecasts for U.S. balancing authorities [eia930about]. Ruggles et al. documented missing and anomalous values and developed screening and multiple imputation [ruggles2020developing]. These procedures are reimplemented in the PUDL data releases used here [pudl2026]. Comparable European resources, Open Power System Data [wiese2019opsd] and the ENTSO-E Transparency Platform, have documented quality limitations [hirth2018entsoe]. Robust-optimisation alternatives to imputation for missing inputs have also been proposed [stratigakos2024resilient]. On evaluation design:

- we use rolling-origin evaluation [tashman2000out] and avoid the common forecasting-evaluation pitfalls catalogued by Hewamalage et al., notably leakage and weak baselines [hewamalage2023forecast];
- we prefer blocked validation schemes for dependent data [bergmeir2012use; bergmeir2018note], extended to spatial and group blocking [roberts2017cross];
- we report scale-free accuracy (MASE) [hyndman2006another];
- we test forecast differences with Diebold-Mariano tests [diebold1995comparing] and block-bootstrap intervals [kunsch1989jackknife; politis1994stationary], following open-benchmark practice in energy forecasting [lago2021forecasting].

**Positioning.** Each element of our study has precedent: cross-region transfer, global gradient boosting, adaptive conformal prediction and operator-forecast data. To our knowledge, based on a non-exhaustive search, they have not been combined into one evaluation across tens of independently operated U.S. balancing authorities. That evaluation would separate geographic from temporal shift, quantify the value of a few weeks of target data, assess interval coverage under both shifts, and benchmark against the operators' own day-ahead forecasts. We contribute this evaluation framework and benchmark, not a new forecasting or conformal algorithm.
